# The Touchline Dribble: marketing and growth strategy

Updated: 27 September 2026. Working six-week plan; review weekly.

## Decisions to preserve

- Audience: global English-speaking football fans.
- Positioning: **Strong opinions on football’s biggest debates—with the evidence and knowledge behind them.**
- Main objective: relevant new readers from search, discussions, and reader sharing; then second-article reads and optional email subscriptions.
- Capacity: one current opinion and one evergreen explainer per week. One story per month replaces an explainer that week if needed.
- Marketing capacity: 3–5 hours weekly, excluding research and writing.
- Instagram: not part of this plan. No carousel or Reel production requirement.
- X: start from 14 followers. Premium is a tool, not a reach guarantee. Do not upgrade or buy advertising to compensate for low reach.
- Keep the existing visual design and football match-minute structure. No homepage poll or dependence on visible community activity.
- Existing articles, stories, saved content, tools, and membership access stay available.

## 1. Website experience

| Full-time · FT | The Weekly Whistle | One inline signup: one strong opinion and one useful lesson each week. |

Hide empty sections rather than inventing content. Monthly stories do not expire merely because a new calendar month begins. Keep publication dates honest.

Remove homepage polling, rumour/fantasy distractions, repeated content lanes, premium/support banners, and contributor recruitment. Keep their existing destinations and footer/secondary links. Club selection must be reader-initiated; do not interrupt first-time article reading with a newsletter overlay.

### Editorial controls

- Set **Reading type** to Opinion or Explainer; do not automatically classify every tactical article as an explainer.
- Add an optional concise verdict, up to three sourced evidence points, a fair counterargument, a related background explainer, and an approved share quote.
- Use homepage curation to select the lead, explainers, latest articles, and archive picks. Earlier sections take priority to prevent duplicates.
- Review archive picks weekly: "older" alone is not a reason to feature something.
- `/learn` links to canonical articles and the existing glossary/tactics resources. Build toward six reviewed explainers; do not publish placeholders to hit a number.
- Preserve paid access rules. Growth work does not authorise changing membership entitlements.

## 2. Content strategy

### One debate, one useful question

Pair a timely opinion with a durable explanation. Examples below are formats, not claims about current matches:

| Current opinion | Supporting knowledge |
| --- | --- |
| The striker is being blamed for a supply problem | What does an isolated striker mean? |
| This transfer fills the wrong need | How do player roles affect squad building? |
| The midfield change solved one problem and created another | How does a double pivot work? |

Before choosing a topic, establish:
1. Is there an actual discussion or reader question?
2. Do I have a defensible angle, example, or original observation?
3. Can I support factual claims with sources?
4. Is there a relevant existing article to update instead of duplicating it?
5. What should a reader understand after reading?

Choose one editorial theme for a two-week cycle, based on what you can cover well. Stay globally relevant without trying to cover every club.

### Opinion structure

- Specific claim in the title; no unsupported outrage.
- Clear verdict near the start.
- Essential context for a new reader.
- Two or three examples or evidence points.
- Strongest reasonable counterargument.
- What would change your view.
- One background explainer and one related perspective where available.

### Explainer structure

Answer a specific question directly, explain the concept, give a concrete example, show an original diagram or worked example when helpful, clarify limitations, and link to a relevant current opinion. Do not use a short definition as a substitute for a useful article.

### Monthly story

Publish one researched story each month. Keep it prominent at half-time with its publication month. During story week, replace the normal explainer if needed and use the story as the main marketing material. No daily story updates are required.

## 3. Distribution without Instagram

### X: a four-week discovery experiment

Prepare three original text-first posts weekly:
- One clear opinion with a reason.
- One concrete example or evidence post.
- One explainer or fair counterargument.

Pin a short introduction plus your strongest analysis. Profile bio should clearly state the site's promise and link to the website. Follow a focused list of 15–20 relevant writers, analysts, and fan accounts whose work you actually read.

Spend 15 minutes on four days reading and contributing to relevant conversations. Replies should add an observation, explanation, or respectful counterargument. Do not paste the same reply or attach a promotional link to every contribution.

Example reply structure:
> I read the problem differently: [claim]. In [specific example], [observable detail]. That suggests [interpretation].

Provide a complete insight in the post. Link to the article when it supplies useful additional evidence. Do not assume links are automatically penalised, or that Premium guarantees distribution. Judge the experiment using actual conversations and site visits.

### Search: compound useful coverage

Research one narrow reader question per explainer using current search results and available Search Console queries. Check search intent before writing. Review titles for clarity, maintain clean canonical URLs, and link opinions and explanations together.

Do not mass-produce thin topic pages. Do not promise rankings. Search may take longer than this six-week evaluation window.

### Two communities

Choose two football communities you would participate in even without owning a blog. Check their rules on ownership disclosures, links, and self-promotion.

Contribute one substantial, self-contained explanation or discussion weekly, with a small number of thoughtful replies as time permits. A blog link is optional and only appropriate when relevant and allowed. No automatic subreddit selection, mass cross-posting, or unsolicited bulk messages.

### Small publisher relationships

From week three, identify one relevant football writer, fan publication, podcast, or newsletter weekly. Read their work before proposing anything.

Offer a specific contribution: an opposing-view paragraph, a guest explanation, an original diagram, or a jointly answered football question. Prepare up to two personalised proposals across the first six weeks. Do not promise reciprocal promotion or send generic "please share my blog" requests.

Outreach template:
> I enjoyed your piece on [specific subject], especially [detail]. I've been researching [related question] and have [specific contribution]. Would an original [guest paragraph/diagram/contrasting viewpoint] be useful for your coverage?

This is a draft template, not authorisation for automated sending.

### Weekly email

Send to opted-in readers only. Use the existing website newsletter as the primary list:
- This week's argument: short summary and a descriptive article link.
- One thing worth understanding: explainer or monthly story.
- One question inviting replies.

Email retains readers; it is not the initial source of discovery. If Substack is already active, reuse the same research for a companion rather than creating a separate editorial workload. Follow the project's blog-to-Substack workflow for publishable skill-generated articles.

## 4. Weekly workload and six-week rollout

| Marketing work, excluding writing | Minutes/week |
| --- | ---: |
| Three X posts | 40 |
| Relevant X discussions | 60 |
| Community participation | 40 |
| Search/topic research | 30 |
| Weekly email | 20 |
| Partnership research and results review | 30 |
| **Total** | **220 (3h 40m)** |

Move sessions around matches and your availability. There is no mandatory posting hour or daily content quota. On a busy week, keep the two articles and useful participation; skip extra promotional assets.

| Week | Website/editorial focus | Distribution focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Verify analytics; simplify interruptions; record baseline | Align X bio and pinned post; identify two communities and relevant accounts |
| 2 | Match-minute homepage and opinion structure | Begin three X posts/week and useful participation |
| 3 | Classify/review explainers; populate Learn | Begin small-publisher research; use search questions to choose subjects |
| 4 | Monthly story and useful sharing links | Promote the story through its argument, one detail, and one takeaway |
| 5 | Refresh archive selections and related links | Repeat themes that attract relevant readers; prepare a specific collaboration proposal |
| 6 | Check the whole reading journey | Review channels, subjects, and workload; set the next six-week plan |

### Six-week output targets

Targets describe work you control, not traffic promises:
- 12 core publishing slots: normally six opinions and six explainers. Each monthly story may replace an explainer slot.
- One story for each monthly edition due during the period; do not force two stories into a short calendar boundary.
- 18 X posts derived from the core articles.
- Up to six useful community contributions, only where welcome.
- One weekly email when there is worthwhile new material and opted-in readers.
- Up to two personalised collaboration proposals.
- Six reviewed explainers in Learn over time, including improved existing work.

## 5. Measurement and review rules

Record the previous 28 days if reliable data exists; otherwise establish a fresh baseline. Never invent baseline numbers.

### Weekly scorecard

| Metric | Baseline | This week | What changed? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Search Console clicks to articles/explainers | | | |
| Attributed visitors from X | | | |
| Attributed visitors from communities/partners | | | |
| Engaged sessions by landing page | | | |
| Sessions with a second article read | | | |
| Newsletter signups | | | |
| Marketing time spent | | | |

Use attribution parameters consistently:
- `utm_source`: x, reddit, newsletter, or named partner.
- `utm_medium`: social, community, email, or referral.
- `utm_campaign`: article slug or newsletter edition.
- `utm_content`: opinion_post, evidence_post, explainer_post, or newsletter_link.

Keep canonical URLs clean. Copy-link interactions do not prove that somebody shared, and share-button clicks do not count as acquired visitors. Some visits will remain unattributed; avoid false precision.

### Review decisions

- Conversations but few visits: improve the profile, pinned piece, and reason to read the full article.
- Visits but little engaged reading: check whether the article delivers the social post's promise.
- Engaged reading but few second reads: improve relevant background/another-perspective links.
- Repeatedly weak X results after four consistent weeks: reduce X time and shift it to communities or relationships.
- Search impressions growing but clicks low: review query intent and title clarity; do not rewrite blindly from a tiny sample.
- Do not declare SEO unsuccessful after four or six weeks solely because clicks are low.
- Do not optimise solely for followers, impressions, or poll responses.
- Expand one activity with evidence of useful results before adding another channel.

## 6. Boundaries and later ideas

No paid ads, bought engagement, automatic outreach, automatic social publishing, Instagram workload, or traffic guarantees. Do not launch daily briefings, transfer trackers, prediction leagues, new games, or a standalone community during this phase.

A later poll needs a clear editorial purpose and a realistic audience; do not add it simply to fill half-time. Revisit Instagram only when production capacity changes.
